What is inside

Pick a section or press a number from 1 to 6.

A browser extension without surprises

The Firefox and Chrome extension fills logins, types 2FA codes and saves new passwords. Passkeys live in the vault in the KeePassXC format.

  • Every new site is confirmed in a Keywarden window
  • Hidden fields and foreign frames are never filled
  • A generator right in the sign-up field
More about the extension

2FA codes next to the password

A codes page like an authenticator app, with the next code shown ahead of time. Add one from a QR in the clipboard or an image, an otpauth link or a secret.

  • 2FAS import and export
  • A QR for your phone in one click
  • Ctrl+T copies the selected entry's code
More about 2FA codes

SSH keys and servers

The built-in SSH agent serves keys from the vault to ssh, git and IDEs while the vault is open. Servers with their address, ProxyJump and port knocking live in the vault and travel to your other computers.

  • Move ~/.ssh and ~/.ssh/config into the vault
  • kw ssh: pick a server in the terminal, by folder
  • Sign git commits with a vault key
More about SSH

Sync through your own folder

Keywarden watches the vault file. Edits from your phone or another computer merge by themselves, Syncthing conflict copies too.

  • Merges entry by entry, not the whole file
  • A mass deletion waits for your confirmation
  • A sync journal with undo
More about sync

Weak, reused, old and expired passwords in one report. A Have I Been Pwned breach check at the press of a button, without sending the passwords themselves.

  • k-anonymity for the breach check
  • “Exclude from reports”, as in KeePassXC

What is protected, and what is not

No big words: exactly what Keywarden does, and where its job ends.

✓Protected

  • The whole vault fileA stolen laptop or a leaked sync folder only gives away an encrypted file.
  • Memory and the clipboardSecrets are wiped on lock and kept out of swap and core dumps; the clipboard clears after 30 seconds.
  • Websites and appsThe extension fills only the entry's own domain, and only after you have confirmed a new site.
  • The vault during crashesA backup before every write and atomic saving: pulling the plug will not corrupt the file.

!Not protected

  • A forgotten master passwordNobody can recover it, us included. Keep a copy of the vault and the password written down somewhere safe.
  • Malware on your systemA program running with your rights while the vault is open can read what you can.
  • A weak passwordArgon2 slows guessing down, but “123456” is still “123456”. Password health shows weak and reused ones.

Same file, different approach. KeePassXC is more mature and proven over years. Keywarden wins on the keyboard and the details: a launcher over any window, a 2FA codes page, SSH servers in the vault. You can use both, taking turns.

Pick Bitwarden if you need sharing with family or a team and a native phone app. Pick Keywarden if you do not want your vault living on anyone's server.

1Password is polished and can share passwords, but it is a subscription, closed source and their server. Keywarden is free and keeps everything with you in an open format.

Questions

Did not find your answer? All questions in the docs, or ask on GitHub.

1Will my KeePassXC vault open?

Yes, with no conversion. Keywarden writes the same KDBX 4, and KeePassXC keeps opening it. You can use both, taking turns.

2What happens if I stop using Keywarden?

Nothing is lost: the file stays an ordinary KeePass vault that any KeePass client opens. There is no lock-in.

3Why no cloud?

So that your passwords do not sit on someone else's server that can be breached or shut down. Sync is done by a folder you choose, and only the encrypted file goes there.

4Are Windows and macOS ready?

They work, but are still in beta: the basics are tested, while some features (YubiKey, a menu bar icon on macOS) are still being worked in. On Linux everything is tested.

5How much does it cost?

Nothing. Keywarden is GPL-3.0 with open source code, and has no subscriptions or paid features.
